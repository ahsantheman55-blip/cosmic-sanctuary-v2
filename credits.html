This page has two parts: the sounds of the Soundscape, then the texts of the Cosmologist Library.

The space recordings and data sonifications come from NASA (including the Chandra X-ray Center, the Jet Propulsion Laboratory and the Scientific Visualization Studio). NASA material is not protected by copyright in the United States; the Chandra X-ray Center states that no claim to copyright is asserted for Chandra material and asks for acknowledgement, which is given here. NASA does not endorse this app. One recording is from LIGO under a Creative Commons Attribution licence. The singing bowls are from Freesound under Creative Commons Zero or Attribution licences.

Every recording was cut, loudness matched and encoded as MP3 for this app. In the app, recordings are played in random sections, with crossfades, panning, filtering, pitch drift and echo.

Synthesised in the app

No recordings are used for these.

  • Primordial hiss and plasma roar: filtered white, pink and brown noise with random flicker.
  • Expanding drones: low saw and triangle chords that glide slowly downwards.
  • First light shimmer: high sine tones fading in and out.
  • Dark-ages sub-bass: sine tones near 40 hertz with a slow swell.
  • First-star ignition swells: rising, brightening chords.
  • Supernova blooms: a falling boom with an opening noise bloom.
  • Galaxy-merger beating: two slightly detuned tones drifting together.
  • Black-hole rumble: very low filtered noise and a sine near 30 hertz.
  • Planet-forming grit: thousands of tiny filtered noise grains that cluster.
  • Pulsar clicks: click trains at the real spin rates of the Vela and Crab pulsars.
  • The CMB chord: three tones in the proportions of the first three acoustic peaks measured by Planck.
  • Binaural beats, isochronic pulses, solfeggio tones, pink, brown and white noise: all generated in the app.
